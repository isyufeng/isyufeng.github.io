About

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Queen Mary University of London. My research focuses on trustworthy language systems, spanning information verification, evidence retrieval, and, more recently, memory and evaluation for LLM agents. I am particularly interested in how language-model-based systems maintain state over time, retrieve the right evidence, and make reliable decisions when information changes across interactions.

Before starting my PhD, I spent three years in industry building production ML platforms, payment infrastructure, and back-end systems. This background shapes how I approach research: I care about both experimental validity and whether a system can be implemented, evaluated, and operated reliably in practice.

Featured Research

Interactive Memory Agent Benchmark | Queen Mary University of London
2026 – Present

An ongoing benchmark for evaluating what memory-enabled LLM agents choose to store, revise, retrieve, and act on during multi-session interactions.

  • Designed a strict-online evaluation protocol in which agents observe only recent dialogue, update persistent memory, retrieve bounded memory records, and then answer or clarify.
  • Developed counterfactual families that present the same query under different prior memory states, isolating failures in state maintenance, retrieval, and clarification decisions.
  • Built paired metrics and calibration baselines to expose trivial strategies such as storing everything, storing nothing, or always asking for clarification; current work is expanding the pilot and validating annotations.
